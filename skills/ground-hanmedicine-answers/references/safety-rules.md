# Safety Rules

## Scope

Apply these rules to patient-specific symptoms, formulas, herbs, acupuncture, cupping, manual therapy, devices, exercise prescriptions, pregnancy, children, older adults, polypharmacy, and organ impairment.

## Required Checks

- Identify urgent red flags before elective treatment discussion.
- Check pregnancy and lactation, age, allergies, current medicines and supplements, liver and kidney disease, bleeding risk, and major comorbidities when relevant.
- Distinguish screening considerations from a diagnosis.
- State when physical examination, laboratory testing, imaging, or conventional evaluation is needed.
- Surface known contraindications and interaction uncertainty.

## Specificity Boundary

- Public-facing answers: do not provide individualized herbal doses, needling depth/angle, bloodletting instructions, or device parameters.
- Clinician-facing briefs: provide evidence-linked candidate parameters only when explicitly requested; label missing patient data and jurisdictional constraints.
- Never turn insufficient information into a complete prescription merely to fill a template.
- Do not infer current safety from historical use or a classical passage.

## Language and Privacy

Avoid guarantees such as “safe,” “effective,” “cures,” or “prevents recurrence” without appropriately strong evidence. Minimize patient data and remove identifiers before using search or external tools.
