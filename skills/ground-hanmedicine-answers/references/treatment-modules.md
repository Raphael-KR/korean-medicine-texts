# Treatment Modules

Load only the requested module. Every module is optional; do not use one merely because a disease term appeared.

## Herbal Medicine

- Required assessment: symptom pattern, tongue/pulse when available, constitution, current medicines and supplements, allergies, pregnancy, hepatic and renal status.
- Output: candidate strategy, formula provenance, study-specific evidence, modification rationale, contraindications, interactions, and missing data.
- Search the local corpus for provenance; use modern external evidence for effectiveness and safety.
- Preserve established formula names when provenance matters. Do not invent a formula or erase its historical identity.
- Provide exact doses only for an explicitly clinician-facing request with adequate context and sources.

## Acupuncture

- Check indication, red flags, bleeding risk, pregnancy, implanted devices, infection risk, anatomy, and practitioner qualifications.
- Report evidence-linked point strategy or protocol, comparator, schedule, adverse events, and uncertainty.
- Do not provide patient-facing needling depth, angle, or self-needling instructions.

## Cupping and Bloodletting

- Check anticoagulation, bleeding disorders, skin integrity, infection, anemia, and consent.
- Separate dry cupping, wet cupping, and bloodletting evidence.
- Do not provide procedural instructions for unsupervised use.

## Manual and Physical Therapy

- Screen trauma, neurological deficits, fracture risk, vascular risk, and systemic disease.
- Distinguish mobilization, manipulation, exercise, and passive modalities.
- Report practitioner dependence and adverse-event evidence.

## Exercise, Food, Qigong, and Prevention

- Match recommendations to diagnosis status, function, comorbidities, and baseline capacity.
- Separate general health guidance from treatment claims.
- Avoid food–herb and food–drug interaction assumptions without sources.

## Mental and Emotional Care

- Screen acute safety concerns and severe functional impairment.
- Do not replace qualified mental-health care with traditional explanatory models.
- Present adjunctive practices as adjunctive when that is what the evidence supports.
